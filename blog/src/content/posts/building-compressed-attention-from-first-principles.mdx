### Decoding

<div style={{ display: "flex", gap: "3rem", alignItems: "flex-start" }}>
<div style={{ flex: 1 }}>

**Prefill**

$$
O = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
$$

</div>
<div style={{ flex: 1 }}>

**Decode**

$$
o_t = \sum_{j=0}^{t} \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{q_t \cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) v_j
$$

</div>
</div>


At inference time, only the last token's query $q_t$ is needed, not the full $Q$ matrix. The model is solely trying to produce $o_t$, the attention output for the most recently generated token, which is then passed through the remaining layers to predict the next token. Since a new token is generated at each step, both $q_t$ and $o_t$ change at every iteration.

The key observation is that this is not the case for most of $K$ and $V$. The vectors $k_1, \dots, k_{t-1}$ and $v_1, \dots, v_{t-1}$ are fixed, they depend only on tokens that have already been generated and will not change when a new token is appended. Rather than recomputing and reloading the entire $K$ and $V$ matrices at each step, we can keep them resident in HBM, compute only the new projections $k_t$ and $v_t$ for the newly generated token, and append them to their stored matrix.

This is **KV caching**. It eliminates the redundant recomputation of $K$ and $V$ across decoding steps and critically reduces the memory transfers that would otherwise dominate inference latency. This saving becomes increasingly significant as context windows grow longer, which is now a central requirement for modern agentic workloads.

## 2. The Roofline Model

Consider the matrix multiplication $Z = X \cdot Y$, with $X \in \mathbb{R}^{A \times B}$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C}$, so $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{A \times C}$.

Arithmetic intensity is defined as:

$$
I = \frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{Bytes moved}}
$$

Each entry of $Z$ requires $B$ multiplications and $B-1$ additions, giving $2B - 1 \approx 2B$ operations. Across all $AC$ output entries:

$$
\text{FLOPs} = (2B - 1) \cdot AC \approx 2ABC
$$

In BF16, each element occupies 2 bytes. The total memory traffic for reading $X$, $Y$ and writing $Z$ is:

$$
\text{Bytes} = 2(AB + BC + AC)
$$

The arithmetic intensity is therefore:

$$
I_{\text{matmul}} = \frac{2ABC}{2(AB + BC + AC)} = \frac{ABC}{AB + BC + AC}
$$
